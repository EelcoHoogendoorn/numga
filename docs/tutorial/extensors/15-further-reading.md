# 15. Further reading

Applications of extensors run from mechanics and estimation to optics, electromagnetism, relativity and spin dynamics. The [extensor overview](../../extensors.md) shows a selection, each with its picture and the lines of code that carry it, and the [index of examples](../../../examples/README.md) lists every runnable example.

numga offers more than this tutorial covers. Sparse extensors, which couple many elements through few nonzero maps, and fields of maps are described in the [syntax reference](../../extensor_syntax.md) and the [fields document](../../fields.md). How numga builds an extensor from an expression and evaluates it is described in [internals](../../internals.md), and the identities defining the adjugate, the adjoint, the trace, the contraction and the outermorphism are collected in the [conventions](../../conventions.md).

Extensors were introduced in the foundations of geometric calculus, and their main use since has been in mathematical physics, above all in the gauge theory of gravity, where the fields themselves are linear functions of multivectors. The texts below come from that setting: they define extensors and develop their theory for readers already at home in it. To our knowledge there is as yet no introductory or application-centred text on extensors; for applications, the examples above are the place to look.

- D. Hestenes and G. Sobczyk, *Clifford Algebra to Geometric Calculus: A Unified Language for Mathematics and Physics*, Reidel, 1984. Extensors are defined in Section 3-10, "Tensors"; extensor fields and their differentials in Section 4-1. [Link](https://math.mit.edu/~dunkel/Teach/18.S996_2022S/books/Hestenes-Sobczyk1984_Book_CliffordAlgebraToGeometricCalc.pdf)
- D. Hestenes, "The design of linear algebra and geometry", *Acta Applicandae Mathematicae* 23, 65–93, 1991. Linear maps, their adjoints and outermorphisms written in geometric algebra.
- C. Doran and A. Lasenby, *Geometric Algebra for Physicists*, Cambridge University Press, 2003. Linear functions of multivectors, their adjoints and outermorphisms, and their use in physics.
- A. Lasenby, C. Doran and S. Gull, "Gravity, gauge theories and geometric algebra", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 356, 487–582, 1998. Gravitation written with linear functions of multivectors as its fields.
